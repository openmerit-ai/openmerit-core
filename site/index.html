A few useful details

Fair questions.

Is OpenMerit an agent?+

No. It works with your coding harness, such as Pi. The harness understands your project, builds evaluations, tests models, and carries out authorized changes. OpenMerit coordinates that workflow, records evidence references, and validates structured results and model comparisons. It relies on the harness to perform the work and report honest evidence.

Which harnesses does it work with?+

Pi 0.87 is the only implemented adapter today. OpenMerit’s core and protocol are designed for other harnesses too, but each needs an adapter. Installing OpenMerit doesn’t add support to another coding agent automatically.

Building a harness adapter ↗
Do I need evaluations before I start?+

You don’t need a finished evaluation suite. In an interactive Pi session, setup asks Pi to understand your task, confirm your requirements and permissions, and establish the necessary evaluations and measurements. Those checks need to reflect your actual product. Ordinary coding activity alone doesn’t measure product quality.

Setting up your first task ↗
Does it always choose the cheapest model?+

No. Comparisons use the quality, cost, speed, and other requirements you confirm. Several models may offer useful tradeoffs. OpenMerit checks the harness’s comparison of tested candidates against those requirements; it doesn’t promise one model that is best for every task, or guaranteed savings.

How much evidence does a recommendation need?+

That depends on your task and the sample requirements you confirm during setup. Reliability and consistency need repeated, representative runs. Missing or insufficient required measurements leave a candidate unresolved in the comparison. OpenMerit checks the reported measurements and comparison rules, but doesn’t independently prove that an external evidence source is truthful.

Understanding the evidence ↗
Will it change my model without asking?+

A proposal waits for /openmerit approve unless your policy enables automatic swaps and its required number of verified swaps has been reached. That number can be zero, so check the policy you confirm during setup. After a change, the workflow requires the harness to evaluate the new model and report the result. Model changes are carried out by the harness.

What if a new model performs worse?+

If post-swap evaluation reports a regression and your policy enables rollback, OpenMerit asks the harness to restore the previous model configuration. Otherwise it waits for your decision. Detection depends on the measurements and checks your harness provides; rollback depends on the harness being able to restore that configuration.

Does it keep running when Pi is closed?+

No. The current Pi adapter checks for due work when sessions start and when it receives task or measurement signals. It doesn’t install a background service or wake Pi on its own. Checks that must run while Pi is closed need an external scheduler to start Pi; OpenMerit reports that requirement when your policy requests persistent scheduling.

How scheduled checks work ↗
Can I pause automatic checks?+

The /openmerit pause command has a known limitation in version 0.1.5: some automatic checks can still start after task or measurement signals, even when status says “paused.” The setting also resets when Pi restarts and doesn’t cancel work already underway. Treat it as a partial pause, not a reliable way to stop all OpenMerit work.

Current limitations ↗
Where does my data live?+

OpenMerit stores project state, an append-only audit log, and evidence manifests in .openmerit/. Pi also records OpenMerit requests and results in its session history. Project context and evaluation data may be sent to the model providers, tools, and evidence systems configured in your harness. Credentials are managed by the harness; local OpenMerit storage doesn’t mean all processing stays on your machine.

Read the security notes ↗
What does it cost to run?+

OpenMerit has no subscription fee. Your provider’s usage charges still apply to setup, assessments, and challenger trials. Any paid tools or infrastructure your harness uses may add costs too.

Is the evaluation budget a hard spending limit?+

No. Setup asks you to confirm an evaluation budget, which OpenMerit passes to the harness as a constraint. The harness is responsible for staying within it. OpenMerit doesn’t independently meter provider spending or cut off requests when the budget is reached. Use spending controls from your provider or infrastructure where available.

Is OpenMerit ready for production?+

It’s in early development and intended for supervised product trials. Automated tests and Pi loading checks cover the implementation, but they don’t establish reliability or better model choices for your workload. Start with a representative task, inspect the evaluations and evidence, and validate a supervised swap and rollback before relying on automation.
