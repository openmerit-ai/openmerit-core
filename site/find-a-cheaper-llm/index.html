How to find a cheaper LLM that works as well as the one your app uses

Test cheaper models on your own task, not on a leaderboard. OpenMerit’s Scout finds each LLM call in your code, writes an eval for what that call has to do, searches cheaper models against it and proposes a switch only when a cheaper model passes everything your current model passes.

Why leaderboards aren’t enough

A benchmark score tells you a model is capable in general. It doesn’t tell you whether it returns the JSON fields your parser expects, follows your refund policy or calls your tools in the right order. Only a test of your own task shows that.

How Scout searches

  • Candidates: models on the endpoints you allow, such as OpenRouter, OpenAI or another OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Each has to cost less per token than your current model and support what the task needs, such as tool calls, images or structured output.
  • Search: Scout ranks candidates by the Epoch Capabilities Index, then binary-searches that ranking on your eval. It screens about log₂ of the pool, not every model.
  • Qualifying: a model qualifies only if it passes at least as many eval runs as your current model and loses no case your current model always gets right.
  • Saving: a switch has to cost at least 10% less per call. A smaller difference is within measurement noise, so Scout keeps your current model.
  • Spending: evals run on your keys. The default budget is $10 per run, and nothing runs until you approve it.

What you get

The search path, the top three candidates beside your current model with their passes, cost per call and latency, and the eval runs behind every number. When you say yes, Scout changes the model name on that one line of your code.

Limits

  • A cheaper model that the ranking places out of order can be missed.
  • An eval every model passes can’t tell them apart. Scout says when that happens.

Try it

Running OpenMerit on your machine is free:

npm install -g openmerit
cd your-repo && openmerit setup && openmerit scout
